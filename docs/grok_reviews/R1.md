# Grok R1 (cheap realistic lighting): appraisal

Delivered 2026-09-26, second attempt (the first stopped for missing files). Appraised by Claude against the code. Grok's text isn't kept; this note is the record of what's worth keeping.

**Grade: fair.**
- Well organised, and it kept to its facts.
- Two of its six options are things the game already does; one rests on a setting that doesn't exist; one is physically wrong.
- It said nothing about the golf-ball look or the noon patches that prompted the task.
- It didn't mark anything as an assumption, though several points were assumptions (see the table).

## Claims checked against the code

| Grok said | True? | What the code says |
|---|---|---|
| Sky view could reach 24–32 blocks at build time for "well under a millisecond per chunk" | Partly | The bake reads column heights copied with a margin of 10 blocks around the chunk (`GROUND_TOPS_PAD`, groundmesh.h). A longer reach needs a bigger copy on the main thread: at 32 blocks, 80 × 80 heights, about 13 KB per chunk instead of 2.6 KB. Workable, but not free, and not where Grok placed the cost. |
| Unloaded ground would count as open, so a corner could brighten when its neighbour loads | Yes | Missing columns read as height −1 (`CopyGroundCells`), so they count as open sky. The chunk is rebuilt when its neighbours change, so it corrects itself, as Grok said. |
| A bent normal fits in "two extra bytes" of the corner | No | The ground vertex is exactly 16 bytes, with no spare byte, and a test enforces it (`static_assert`, groundmesh.h). A bent normal means a 20-byte vertex (+25% vertex memory and bandwidth) or giving something else up. Grok asked this as an open question; it should have been marked as an assumption in the recommendation. |
| Option 2: "replace the current fixed colours" with time-of-day tables | False premise | The colours already follow the time of day: `ComputeAtmosphere` (sky.h) sets the sun colour from the sun's height (white-gold high, orange low), the sky and ambient from daylight, a twilight band, moonlight and exposure, every frame, on the CPU. No tables or constant buffers are needed; better curves would just be edits there. |
| Option 4: darken direct light by openness at a low sun | Mostly exists | Direct sun is already scaled by openness at every sun height: `sunLit × (0.55 + 0.45 × ao)` (render.cpp PSMain). Making it stronger at a low sun is what gave lumpy ground its dimpled look under SSAO. Risky. |
| Option 5: "the cloud shadow texture" and "the cloud quality setting that already exists" | False | There's no cloud shadow texture (the shader works out each cloud shadow per pixel, `CumulusDensity`) and no cloud quality setting. |
| Option 5: ambient lifts under a cloud | Wrong physics | A cloud blocks the sun; it doesn't add light underneath. Shade under a cloud is lit by the rest of the sky, which the ambient already covers. |
| Option 6: bake curvature | Mostly exists | Openness is already a measure of how enclosed a corner is (solid cells among the 64 around it), which is what concave versus convex amounts to at this scale. A second value would largely repeat it, and there's no spare byte. |

## Verdicts

| Option | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Longer horizon for the sky view | **Adapt** | Worth doing: large landforms would shade the ground around them, the thing the current 8-block reach misses. Take the reach only (16–24 blocks, with the bigger copy measured), not the bent normal, unless we decide a 20-byte vertex is worth it. Caution: the noon patches may come from per-corner baking, and leaning harder on it could make them worse. Check with PLAN_LOOK item G first. |
| 2. Colour tables for the day | **Adapt, small** | The mechanism exists. The useful part is reviewing the colour curves in `ComputeAtmosphere` against real skies (dawn warmth, cooler midday shade). A tuning pass, not a system. |
| 3. Height-aware aerial perspective | **Keep for later** | Sound and cheap: a few instructions in the fog function. It must keep the world's edge fading fully into the sky (D64). Small value at render distance 3; more once we can see further. |
| 4. Openness darkens a low sun | **Drop** | Already there in milder form. Stronger is the route back to the golf ball. |
| 5. Cloud-underside bounce | **Drop** | Rests on things that don't exist, and the physics is wrong. |
| 6. Curvature bake | **Drop** | Duplicates openness, and there's no room in the vertex. |

## What we keep

1. **A longer sky-view reach** (Option 1, reach only). It goes after item G, once we know whether per-corner baking causes the noon patches.
2. **A tuning pass on the day's colours** in `ComputeAtmosphere` (Option 2, reframed).
3. **Height-aware haze** (Option 3), parked until we can see further.

None of these is scheduled. They join PLAN_LOOK's list for the owner to choose from.
