# Grok R4 (our own look): appraisal

Delivered 2026-09-26. Appraised by Claude against the code; Grok's text isn't kept.

**Grade: weak to fair.**
- Much of it repeats what the game already does (light colours through the day, two cloud layers, haze).
- Some of it pulls against what the owner asked for in the same week: flat colour fields and pixel-crisp clouds, where the owner wants textures with more life and realistic, flowing clouds.
- Two ideas are worth keeping.

## Claims checked against the code

| Grok said | True? | What the code says |
|---|---|---|
| Palette tints applied "once on the background thread when a cell is built" | Misread | Colour comes from the material textures in the shader. There's no per-vertex colour and no spare byte (the ground vertex is 16 bytes, full). A palette would be a per-material tint in the material constants: free per frame, but not a build-time step. |
| Facet edges: an edge mask "in a thin secondary texture or vertex colour at build time" | Won't fit | No room in the vertex, and a mask texture for every facet isn't a thing the mesher could make cheaply. The game already has an **outlines** option (the post pass finds edges from depth and normals, off by default). The cheap test is to turn it on and tune it for facets. |
| "Darkening on the side of a triangle facing away from the sun" | Exists | That's ordinary lighting: each facet is lit by its own flat normal. |
| Light colour ramps through the day | Exists | `ComputeAtmosphere` (sky.h) already does sun colour by height, twilight, moonlight and night ambient. Any work here is tuning, as in R1. |
| Clouds "with the same 32-pixel discipline", "never photoreal" | Conflicts | The owner has just asked for realistic, flowing, changing clouds (PLAN_LOOK A–C). |
| Palette as "flat colour fields", textures near-neutral | Conflicts | The owner wants more dynamism, variation and resolution in textures (PLAN_LOOK E–F), not flatter. |

## Worth keeping

1. **A mark language per material** (lever 5): each material gets its own angular marks, drawn as hard-edged pixel shapes (blades for grass, chips for gravel, bands for sandstone), distinct at a glance. A good brief for redrawing the textures at 64 (PLAN_LOOK F), and it fits TEXTURE_BRIEF's pixel-art rule. Not "flat colour or one hard edge per texel": that's too strict for 64.
2. **Transitions made of pieces, not smears** (lever 6, adapted): where materials meet, near the player the blend is decided **per texel**, so the edge is a crisp mosaic of whole texels from each material rather than a noisy fringe finer than a texel. This slots straight into PLAN_LOOK D (clean material edges) and costs nothing extra. Not a band built on the background thread: the blend is per pixel already.
3. **The "never look like" list**, partly: keep "visible facets always", "no photographic textures", "no featureless night". Drop "no soft transitions", which the owner has asked for in another form.

## Verdicts

| Lever | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 1. Palette | **Adapt, small**: a per-material tint and saturation check when textures are redrawn (slate is too dark, PLAN_LOOK H). |
| 2. Facet edges | **Try the existing outlines setting** before building anything. |
| 3. Day light colours | **Exists**; tuning only. |
| 4. Sky character | **Drop**: conflicts with the owner's clouds. |
| 5. Texture style | **Keep, as a brief for redrawing the textures**. |
| 6. Transition bands | **Adapt**: the per-texel mosaic edge, folded into PLAN_LOOK D. |
| 7. Haze and edges with distance | **Covered** by R1 option 3 (height haze). |
