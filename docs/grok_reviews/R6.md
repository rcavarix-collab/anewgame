# Grok R6 (caves): appraisal

Delivered 2026-09-26. Appraised by Claude against the code; Grok's text isn't kept.

**Grade: good.** The best of the batch:
- The three approaches are genuinely different and compared on the same points.
- It understood the chunk-skipping walk correctly.
- It stayed within its facts.

Its cost figures are estimates, which it should have labelled as such.

## Claims checked against the code

| Grok said | True? | What the code says |
|---|---|---|
| A sealed cave inside a chunk still blocks the visibility walk; only mouths that reach a chunk face open it | True | Chunk openings are which faces connect through open cells (`FacetOpenings`, facetmesh.h). A sealed pocket connects no faces. |
| Caves need a new terrain version; old worlds untouched | True | Terrain versions are frozen (terrain.h; v1 and v2 pinned by fingerprint tests). A caves version would be v3. |
| The generator fills whole columns, so caves fit the column loop | True | `HillsColumn` fills every cell of a column (`TerrainColumn::cells`), not just a height, so carving air inside it needs no new contract with the mesher. The mesher already handles any shape of cells, overhangs included. |
| 3-D noise per block: 6–20× today's 0.05 ms, "on a GTX 1060 class CPU core" | Estimate; mixed up | It's a processor cost (a graphics card isn't involved), paid on the job threads while columns stream in, not per frame. The standard trick Grok mentions briefly settles it: sample the density on a coarse lattice (every 4 blocks) and interpolate. That's 16–64× fewer samples, putting Approach A near Approach C's cost. |
| Worms: adjacent columns "may disagree" | Understated | Worms are defined in world space, so there's no disagreement if every column considers every worm that could reach it. That means each column searches the coarse grid cells around it out to the longest worm's reach. That bound is the real design constraint, and it sets the cost. |
| Caves would be lit by sun and moon near their mouths | True, and more | Direct sun reaches in through the shadow map. The sky light is baked from column heights (`GroundSkyAt`), so anything under a roof counts as walled in and stays dark, as DESIGN 23.4 intends. Deep caves are dark by design; lighting them needs glowing blocks (4.12) or a later light source. |

## Verdicts

| Approach | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A. 3-D density noise | **Keep as an option**, on a coarse lattice with interpolation. It gives the natural chambers and overhangs that worms don't. |
| B. 2-D mask with two heights | **Drop**: flat roads under hills; the look is too artificial for the cost saved. |
| C. Sparse worms | **Keep**: long readable tunnels, and it culls well. Its reach bound needs designing. |
| Recommendation (C first, A later) | **Adapt**: likely both together, worms for tunnels and coarse density for the occasional chamber and overhang, measured on the job threads. |

Scope: caves are not a scope row yet; they'd come with a terrain v3 decision by the owner (see R7).
